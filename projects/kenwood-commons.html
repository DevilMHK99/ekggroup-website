The project

A dormant parcel, brought back into use.

Kenwood Commons sits on the largest contiguous undeveloped parcel in the City of Albany, land that has stood idle for years. The plan is to return it to productive use as a single, coherent, mixed-use community.

The master plan carries a major workplace alongside 264 new homes, research space, and the public realm and amenity that let a neighborhood function. A headquarters building or a campus of several can sit on the Route 9W frontage, with the housing and green space set behind it across the 76-acre site.

Why this site

The site sits less than a mile from downtown Albany and the Capitol, about a minute from I-787 and I-87, with frontage and signage on Route 9W. For an employer moving several hundred or several thousand people, that is a short commute from everywhere in the Capital Region.

Office is permitted under the current zoning on the 9W frontage, so a workplace here is reviewed through site plan approval rather than a rezoning. The plateaus are flat and buildable within a wooded campus, with walking trails planned and wetlands protected, and utilities are already on site: power, city water and natural gas.

It is flexible by design. A single headquarters building or a campus of several, including a historic five-storey building restored as part of it, with parking structured or at grade. A cafe and retail can sit on site, with new homes planned nearby, so it works as a place to live as well as to work.

Built with the community

We have approached Kenwood Commons as a long-term member of the Albany community rather than a passing developer. The project is designed to deliver new housing, hundreds of construction and permanent jobs, and a durable tax base for a city that has carried this parcel as a liability for too long.

That work is being done in the open, through the City Planning Board, the state grid operator and Albany’s economic-development authorities, and in direct conversation with residents and local organizations.

Where it stands

The current focus at Kenwood Commons is design, engineering and community engagement, ahead of formal applications.
